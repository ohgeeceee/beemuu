Community repair opinions

The same code means different work depending on who is paying. These are the recorded DIY, independent-shop and dealer paths:

PerspectiveActionCostTimeDifficulty
DIYClean the MAF sensor and smoke test the intake tract for vacuum leaks
Bimmerpost / N54Tech forums		free30 minutesEasy
The N55 charge pipe elbow and PCV valve are the two most common vacuum leak sources. A can of MAF cleaner and a visual inspection often finds the problem for free.
Independent shopSmoke test the intake tract, check fuel trims under load, inspect charge pipe and valve cover gasket
Independent BMW shop experience		~$1501-2 hoursModerate
If smoke test finds no leak, check the HPFP rail pressure under load. N55 HPFPs fail around 80k-120k miles and can cause lean codes only under high demand.
DealerReplace DME control unit and re-flash with latest ISTA software
BMW TSB B01 01 22		~$24002-3 daysAdvanced
Dealer approach is to replace the DME if no obvious vacuum leak is found. Under warranty this is covered; out of warranty it is cost-prohibitive.

Frequently asked

Is code 29E0 safe to clear?

Clearing is only safe once you have read and recorded the freeze frame. Re-appearing after a clear is the useful signal; clearing without recording destroys it.

Is 29E0 a BMW-specific or a generic code?

This is a BMW manufacturer hex code. A generic OBD-II tool will not show it — you need BMW-protocol access over K+DCAN or ENET/DoIP.
