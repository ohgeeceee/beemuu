# Plan — v4.2.0 "Cross-Check"

**Parent:** `docs/v3.8_to_v5.0_roadmap.md` (schedule of record)
**Tier:** A — pure analysis over a recorded log, plus a read-only panel.
**Depends on:** v3.8.0 (channels are only cross-checked once they are believed),
v3.9.0 (cycle boundaries give the checks somewhere to start).

## Why

The v3 engines read channels one at a time. But physical quantities in a car are
**not independent**: intake air and manifold pressure move with rpm, coolant and
oil warm together, lambda spends most of its life near 1. When two channels that
must agree stop agreeing, one of them is wrong — and the useful question is
*which*. That is the cross-check, and it is the first feature in the product
that can say a specific sensor is lying rather than merely odd.

## Slices

### S1 — Alignment
`align(a, b, opts)` — resample two channels onto a shared time grid, pairing
only samples within `MAX_SKEW_S`. Returns `{ pairs: [{x, a, b}], used, skipped }`.
Channels that cannot be aligned (`skipped` exceeds the usable share) yield an
`unknown` result rather than a low-confidence guess.

### S2 — Relationship primitives
- `sign(n)` — the expected direction of a pair, or `0` for "no expected sign".
- `correlation(pairs)` — Pearson r over the aligned pairs, guarded against a
  zero-variance side (that returns `null`, not `NaN` leaking into a verdict).
- `residualTrend(pairs, model)` — is the disagreement growing, constant, or
  flipping sign across the log? A disagreement that only appears hot is a
  different fault from one present from the first sample.

### S3 — The pair table
Named pairs with their expected relationship and tolerance: `rpm ↔ speed`,
`maf ↔ rpm`, `map ↔ maf`, `coolant ↔ oil`, `lambda ↔ fuel_trim`,
`boost ↔ rpm`. Each pair carries a rationale string the UI can show, so a
finding is never an unexplained number.

### S4 — `checkPair(nameA, nameB, pointsA, pointsB, opts)`
`{ pair, verdict: 'agree' | 'disagree' | 'unknown', score, confidence,
suspect, correlation, residual_trend, evidence }`.

`suspect` names **one side** and says why — e.g. coolant flat while oil warms
normally points at the coolant channel. When the evidence cannot separate the
two sides the verdict is `disagree` with `suspect: null` and an evidence line
saying the pair is inconsistent but the sides cannot be told apart. **Guessing
a culprit is the failure mode this module exists to avoid.**

### S5 — `analyze(series, opts)`
Runs every pair that both channels support, and returns
`{ checks, disagreeing, unknown_pairs, summary }`. A pair whose channels are
absent is reported `unknown` with the missing channel named.

### S6 — Panel — "Cross-checks"
Per pair: verdict, the named suspect if there is one, r, and the engine's
evidence. `unknown` pairs are shown with the reason, not hidden.

### S7 — Release cut (stamp → gen → test → push → tag `v4.2.0` → publish → deploy).

## Refusal contract

- A pair that cannot be aligned, has too few overlapping samples, or has a
  zero-variance side is `unknown` with `score: null` — never a weak verdict
  dressed up as a finding.
- The engine never attributes a fault to a side the evidence does not single
  out, and a test pins exactly that (`suspect === null` on a symmetric
  disagreement).

## Acceptance criteria

- `node --test src/js/test/sensor_plausibility.test.cjs` green; full suite green.
- A clean synthetic log reports every supported pair `agree`.
- A frozen coolant channel against a warming oil channel names coolant as the
  suspect.
- A symmetric disagreement produces `disagree` with `suspect: null`.
- Missing channels produce `unknown` with the channel named, not a low score.
- Every threshold used is an exported constant.

## Non-goals

- Modelling engine physics beyond sign and rough magnitude. The checks are
  relationship tests, not a simulation.
- Anything requiring a channel the log does not contain.
