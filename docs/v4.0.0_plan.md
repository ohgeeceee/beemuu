# Plan — v4.0.0 "The Fleet"

**Parent:** `docs/v3.8_to_v5.0_roadmap.md` (schedule of record)
**Tier:** A — pure analysis over garage records, plus a read-only board.
**Depends on:** v3.9.0 (cycles feed the per-vehicle rollup).

## Why this is the major bump

v3 is per-car forensics: everything the app learned to see, it sees *one car at
a time*. The natural next version aggregates exactly that same knowledge across
**all the cars in the garage** and answers the question an owner with three
vehicles actually has: *which one needs me this week?*

A major bump rather than a point release because it is the first view in the
product whose subject is the fleet, not the vehicle — the app-shell, the garage
model and the dossier all get a second consumer.

## Slices

### S1 — Input normalisation
`parseDate(s)` → epoch days or `null`. `daysBetween`, `kmBetween`. A date the
engine cannot parse is a **reported data problem**, never silently dropped and
never guessed at.

### S2 — `serviceItems(vehicle, now)`
Per tracked item:
`{ id, label, last_done_km, last_done_date, due_at_km, due_at_date,
km_remaining, days_remaining, status }` where `status` is one of
`overdue` | `due_soon` | `ok` | `unknown`.
- **`unknown` is the default when an interval is missing.** Nothing is "ok"
  by omission — an item with no interval supplied by the caller is unjudged.
- km-driven and day-driven intervals are evaluated **separately**; the status
  is the worse of the two that are actually answerable.

### S3 — `odometerHealth(vehicle)`
Monotonic? duplicates? out of order? A non-monotonic odometer is surfaced as a
data problem and is **not** silently sorted — sorting it would hide exactly the
thing the reader needs to see.

### S4 — `costProfile(vehicle)`
`{ total, entries, mean, median, outlier_entries }`. Outliers use a robust
median-absolute-deviation rule, and only when there are at least
`MIN_COST_ENTRIES`; below that the engine says the question is unjudged rather
than calling a small sample normal.

### S5 — `gradeVehicle(vehicle, now)` and `fleet(vehicles, now)`
`gradeVehicle` → `{ score, grade, open_dtcs, overdue_count, due_soon_count,
cost_total, reasons, confidence }`.
`fleet` → `{ ...vehicles ranked best-first, summary: { n_vehicles, n_graded,
n_ungraded, urgent, total_cost, headline } }`.
Ungraded vehicles are counted **as ungraded** and are shown with their reasons —
they are not scored at zero, and they are not hidden.

### S6 — Board panel — "Fleet"
Per-vehicle row with grade, overdue/due-soon counts, open DTC count and cost
total; a summary head; ungraded rows rendered plainly as ungraded.

### S7 — Release cut (stamp → gen → test → push → tag `v4.0.0` → publish → deploy).

## Refusal contract

A vehicle with no service history, no odometer, or an unparseable date gets
`grade: null`, `score: null`, and a `reasons` array naming what was missing. The
engine **never invents a service interval the caller did not supply** and never
converts missing data into a passing grade.

## Determinism

`now` is an **injected argument**. There is no `Date.now()` anywhere in the
engine, so every ranking and every interval is reproducible in a test.

## Acceptance criteria

- `node --test src/js/test/fleet_dashboard.test.cjs` green; full suite green.
- Overdue / due_soon / ok flip at exactly the exported boundary constants.
- A vehicle with no history is `ungraded` and appears in the board with its
  reasons — not scored 0, not omitted.
- A non-monotonic odometer is reported, not repaired.
- Ranking is stable and deterministic for a fixed injected `now`.

## Non-goals

- Telematics, live polling or any background refresh. The board renders a
  snapshot the caller hands it.
- Predictive failure modelling — it grades against stated intervals only.
